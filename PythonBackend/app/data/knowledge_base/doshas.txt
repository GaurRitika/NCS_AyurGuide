UNDERSTANDING THE THREE DOSHAS IN AYURVEDA

Ayurveda describes three fundamental bio-energies, called doshas, that
govern all physical and mental processes in the body. Every person has
a unique combination of these three doshas, known as their Prakriti or
constitution. Understanding one's dominant dosha helps guide diet,
daily routine, and lifestyle choices toward better balance and health.

VATA DOSHA
Vata is composed of the elements air and space, and governs movement
in the body, including breathing, blood circulation, and nerve
impulses. People with a dominant Vata constitution tend to have a thin
frame, dry skin, and cold hands and feet. They are naturally quick in
thought and speech, creative, and enthusiastic, but under stress they
can become anxious or find it hard to stay grounded. Vata types
usually have variable digestion and light, easily disrupted sleep. To
balance Vata, warm, cooked, and moist foods are recommended, along
with regular daily routines, warmth, and calming practices such as oil
massage and gentle yoga.

PITTA DOSHA
Pitta is composed of the elements fire and water, and governs
digestion, metabolism, and energy production in the body. People with
a dominant Pitta constitution often have a medium build, warm skin,
and a strong appetite with efficient digestion. They tend to be sharp,
focused, and natural leaders, but under stress can become irritable or
prone to anger. Pitta types are sensitive to heat and may experience
skin sensitivity or acidity when imbalanced. To balance Pitta, cooling
foods such as cucumber, coconut, and leafy greens are recommended,
along with avoiding excessive heat, spicy food, and overexertion.

KAPHA DOSHA
Kapha is composed of the elements earth and water, and governs
structure, lubrication, and stability in the body. People with a
dominant Kapha constitution typically have a sturdy build, smooth
skin, and steady digestion. They are naturally calm, affectionate,
and grounded, but under imbalance can become lethargic or prone to
weight gain. Kapha types tend to sleep deeply and have strong stamina
once active. To balance Kapha, light, warm, and spiced foods are
recommended, along with regular physical activity and avoiding
excessive sleep or heavy, oily foods.

MAINTAINING DOSHA BALANCE
No dosha is inherently good or bad; imbalance, not the dosha itself,
causes discomfort or illness. Most individuals have one or two
dominant doshas rather than all three equally. Seasonal changes,
diet, stress, and daily habits can shift doshas out of balance
temporarily. Ayurveda emphasizes personalized adjustments to diet and
routine, based on one's unique constitution, as the primary tool for
restoring and maintaining balance.
